The privacy gap in cloud meeting tools

Most meeting tools work by sending your recording to their servers. That means the conversations where salaries, firings, legal questions, money, and deals get discussed are stored on somebody else's computers, under somebody else's account rules — and you keep paying every month for access to your own history. Stop paying, and that history winds down with the plan.

Local transcription closes that gap by never opening it. The recording stays on your machine, the transcript is a file in a library you own, and nobody at MeetScribe ever sees either one. There is nothing to leak from a server that never received it.

How MeetScribe works locally

  1. Record, or drop in a recording

    Record from your microphone inside MeetScribe, or drop in an audio or video file — MP3, WAV, M4A, MP4, FLAC, WebM. You can also import the transcripts you already have from Fathom, Otter, Fireflies, and Zoom, so your past meetings live in the same library.

  2. It transcribes on your computer

    A speech model that lives on your machine turns the recording into a structured, searchable transcript. No cloud transcription service, no uploads — the recording never leaves your computer.

  3. The follow-up writes itself

    MeetScribe pulls out every action item with its owner and due date, plus every decision, and files them in the commitments register described below.

More than a transcript: the commitments register

A transcript tells you what was said. The register tells you what is still owed.

Every confirmed action item from every meeting lands in one list, split the way you actually think about it: what you owe, and what you're waiting on. Every item carries the exact sentence from the meeting as proof, so "I thought you meant next week" has an answer. Nothing is tracked until you confirm it, and only you ever change a status.

Search your whole history

The honest limits

The speech model doesn't identify who is speaking, so a transcribed recording comes in without speaker labels — a sentence that names a person still gets that person as the owner, but a bare "I will…" stays unassigned rather than guessed at. Accuracy depends on your audio, the same as anywhere: clear recordings in a quiet room do best. Recording and transcription also use two optional packages listed in an included requirements file, plus the free ffmpeg program for non-WAV recordings.
