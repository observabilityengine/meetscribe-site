Who it's for

Your meetings pile up in every shape: a recording on your phone, an MP4 from a call, a Fathom export here, an Otter text file there. And the two things you actually needed from each one — who owes what, and what got decided — sit buried in the middle of page three, if a transcript exists at all.

MeetScribe is for anyone who lives in meetings and wants that pile in one place they own: searchable, deduplicated, transcribed when there's no transcript yet, and summarized into the parts that matter — action items and decisions.

How it works

Three ways in, one library, about five minutes the first time.

  1. Record it, or bring what you have

    Record from your microphone inside MeetScribe, or drop in an audio or video recording — MP3, WAV, M4A, MP4, FLAC, WebM. MeetScribe transcribes it offline, on your machine. Already have transcripts? Export them from Fathom, Otter, Fireflies or Zoom — TXT, VTT, SRT or JSON, any mix. The free guides below walk through each one.

  2. It all lands in one library

    Drop in a whole folder — MeetScribe processes it on your machine, transcribes any recordings it finds, detects duplicates so nothing doubles up, and builds one searchable library on your machine.

  3. Ask it questions — and work the list

    Search by word, speaker, date or source. Or open the commitments register and get every promise across every meeting, split into what you owe and what you're waiting on.

What it does

One library for every meeting you have, in whatever shape it arrives — and the follow-up list writes itself.

Record and transcribe, offline

Record from your microphone, or give MeetScribe a recording file, and it transcribes with a local speech model on your own computer. No cloud transcription service, no uploads — the recording never leaves your machine.

Action items, with owners and due dates

The whole point. "I'll send that Friday" becomes a line in a report: who, what, and the actual calendar date — across all your meetings at once, not buried in one transcript.

Decisions, on the record

Every "let's go with…" and "we decided…" pulled into one list, so "wait, when did we agree to that?" has an answer.

Search across everything

One search box over every meeting in the library — transcribed or imported — with speaker, date and source filters, instead of hunting app by app.

Bulk export

The whole library, or any part of it, out to clean Markdown or text files — for your notes, your docs, or feeding into any other tool.

Duplicates caught for you

Re-import the same folder next week and it skips what it already has. Your library stays clean as it grows.

Private by design

MeetScribe runs locally and offline on your own computer. There are no hosted accounts and no uploads — your recordings and transcripts stay with you.

Every promise, tracked: the commitments register

Extracting action items is where other tools stop. This is where follow-through starts.

Every action item from every meeting flows into one list, split the way you actually think about it: what you owe, and what you're waiting on. Delegation runs in both directions — a flat per-meeting list can't tell them apart. The register can.

The exact words, as proof

Every commitment carries the verbatim sentence from the transcript, with the meeting and the date one step away. When someone says "I never agreed to that," the words are right there.

Nothing tracked without your say-so

New items wait in a confirm queue. You approve each one as-is, fix the owner or due date, edit the wording, or drop it. A sentence that was never really a promise dies in the queue instead of becoming a false obligation — and only you ever change a status.

Overdue and due this week

Dedicated views surface what's late and what's coming up, and undated items get their own triage list instead of quietly vanishing. Due commitments also export to a standard calendar (.ics) file, so the dates live where you already look.

Follow-ups, already drafted

When it's time to chase, MeetScribe drafts the message for you — a first assignment, a gentle nudge, or an overdue reminder — quoting the commitment, its due date, and the proof sentence. You copy it, check it, and send it from your own mail. MeetScribe never sends anything itself.

Questions

Do I need to create an account?

No account. Buy it, install it, use it. MeetScribe runs on your machine — there's nothing to sign into.

Does MeetScribe run online?

No. Recording, transcription, import, search, actions, decisions, the commitments register and export all work offline, on your own computer. There are no hosted accounts and nothing is uploaded. The only downloads are the program itself and a one-time speech-model download (about 75 MB) the first time you transcribe — MeetScribe tells you the size and destination before it starts.

Is it a subscription?

The core tool is $29 one-time — no monthly fee. MeetScribe Pro, which adds automatic Fathom sync, is a separate optional subscription at $9/month.
