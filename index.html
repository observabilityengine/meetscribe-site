Every promise, tracked: the commitments register

Extracting action items is where other tools stop. This is where follow-through starts.

Every action item from every meeting flows into one list, split the way you actually think about it: what you owe, and what you're waiting on. Delegation runs in both directions — a flat per-meeting list can't tell them apart. The register can.

The exact words, as proof

Every commitment carries the verbatim sentence from the transcript, with the meeting and the date one step away. When someone says "I never agreed to that," the words are right there.

Nothing tracked without your say-so

New items wait in a confirm queue. You approve each one as-is, fix the owner or due date, edit the wording, or drop it. A sentence that was never really a promise dies in the queue instead of becoming a false obligation — and only you ever change a status.

Overdue and due this week

Dedicated views surface what's late and what's coming up, and undated items get their own triage list instead of quietly vanishing. Due commitments also export to a standard calendar (.ics) file, so the dates live where you already look.

Follow-ups, already drafted

When it's time to chase, MeetScribe drafts the message for you — a first assignment, a gentle nudge, or an overdue reminder — quoting the commitment, its due date, and the proof sentence. You copy it, check it, and send it from your own mail. MeetScribe never sends anything itself.

The honest limits

MeetScribe works from words and patterns, not minds. It catches commitments phrased the way people usually phrase them — "I'll send that by Friday," "Sarah will book the room" — and it will miss vague ones like "we should probably look into that sometime." Every item it finds quotes the full sentence, so you can check it in context, and it never invents one that isn't in the text.

Transcription has its own limits, stated plainly. The speech model doesn't identify who is speaking, so recordings come in without speaker labels — items that name a person still get that person as owner, but a bare "I will…" from a recording stays unassigned rather than guessed at. And accuracy depends on the audio: clear recordings in a quiet room do best; crosstalk, distant microphones, and names can come out wrong. Read the transcript before you rely on it.

Two practical notes. Recording and transcription use two optional packages listed in an included requirements file (a one-line install), plus the free ffmpeg program for non-WAV recordings. And the first time you transcribe, MeetScribe downloads its speech model once — about 75 MB for the default — tells you the size and destination first, and never touches the network for transcription again.

Questions

Do I need to create an account?

No account. Buy it, install it, use it. MeetScribe runs on your machine — there's nothing to sign into.

Does MeetScribe run online?

No. Recording, transcription, import, search, actions, decisions, the commitments register and export all work offline, on your own computer. There are no hosted accounts and nothing is uploaded. The only downloads are the program itself and a one-time speech-model download (about 75 MB) the first time you transcribe — MeetScribe tells you the size and destination before it starts.

Can it transcribe a recording I already have?

Yes — that's built in. Give it an audio or video file (MP3, WAV, M4A, MP4, FLAC, OGG, WebM) and it transcribes it offline with a local speech model, then treats it like any other meeting: searchable, exportable, and mined for action items and decisions. You can also record straight from your microphone inside the tool.

Does transcription label who is speaking?

No — the model doesn't identify speakers, and MeetScribe won't guess. A transcribed recording comes in as one voice stream. Sentences that name a person ("Marcus will draft…") still get that person as the owner; a bare "I will…" stays unassigned for you to confirm in the register.

What do I need for recording and transcription?

Two optional packages listed in the included requirements file — one install command — plus the free ffmpeg program if you'll transcribe non-WAV files. Everything else (import, search, export, the register) runs on plain Python 3.10+ with nothing extra.

How does the commitments register work?

Every action item MeetScribe finds lands in a confirm queue first — nothing is tracked until you approve or fix it. Confirmed items split into "what you owe" and "what you're waiting on," each with the exact sentence from the meeting as proof. You can see what's overdue or due this week, export due dates to your calendar as an .ics file, and have follow-up messages drafted for you. MeetScribe never sends a message itself — you always send from your own mail.

Is it a subscription?

The core tool is $29 one-time — no monthly fee. MeetScribe Pro, which adds automatic Fathom sync, is a separate optional subscription at $9/month.

Which tools can it import from?

Fathom, Otter, Fireflies, and Zoom — you export transcripts from those tools (TXT, VTT, SRT or JSON) and import them into MeetScribe. Recordings from anywhere come in through transcription instead.

What does it pull out of a meeting?

Action items with owners and due dates, plus a list of the decisions that were made — like the sample above.

What if I import the same meeting twice?

MeetScribe catches duplicates, so your library doesn't fill up with copies.

Does it sync with Fathom automatically?

The core tool doesn't — you export and import, or transcribe the recording. Automatic Fathom sync is what MeetScribe Pro adds, using your own Fathom API key; synced meetings land in the same local library.
